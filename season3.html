The Premise

Abigail arrives at Novatrack with thirty years of institutional literacy behind her — fashion, advertising, two decades of learning exactly how a system hides what it does not want found. She is given real authority and real financial oversight, and within weeks she finds bad debt cycling through the same customer accounts: credited away at month-end, quietly reinvoiced the next, never once written off. She reports it herself, before anyone has to ask.

The Cost

Being right does not protect her. The labor lawyer she hires to defend her position studies Novatrack through her case instead. He keeps her waiting outside his office until her appeal deadline passes, then calls it hopeless. She can see the fraud, the pattern, the evidence, with total precision. What she does not always see is who is standing closest to her.

Characters

The Internal Ally

Sadie Quinn

Call Center Manager, and the closest thing Abi has to a friend inside the building. Makes room at the coffee table on day one and keeps laughing in a place that has forgotten how.

The Honest Story

Nina Caldwell

Marketing Manager. One of the two decent people left in the building, by Sadie's count — turning customer confidence into a narrative the finance department is quietly working to disprove.

The Keeper of Records

Adrian Cole

Security Manager, former police officer. Recognizes the difference between guilt and fear before anyone hands him a file, and preserves the access history nobody else thinks to alter.

The Quiet Exit

Martin Hargreaves

Financial Manager. Builds Novatrack's statements by hand for seven months rather than admit the system is broken. When the investigation closes, he is the only one who leaves on his own terms.

The Lighthouse

Abi Fisher

Executive. Mother. The woman who can read a balance sheet at forty paces and still not see the man standing behind her chair.

Abi arrives at Novatrack with three decades of pattern-reading behind her, and finds the fraud fast — bad debt cycling through the same accounts, credited at month-end, reinvoiced the following month, never quite written off. She follows it herself, account by account, until the pattern is undeniable.

Her gift is clarity turned toward evidence. Documents, transactions, contradictions — she reads them the way a lighthouse reads the water directly ahead of it.

Her blind spot is the same beam. What stands just behind her, in the dark beneath the light, she does not always think to check.

She hires a lawyer to protect her. She should have investigated him first.

The Financial Director

Everett Hale

Controls the financial story completely, and is never quite the one who has to explain it. Instructs the practice that makes bad debt disappear, then builds the case against the person who found it.

The Credit Controller

Chloe Whitaker

Performs the credit irregularities and benefits from them personally, hiding behind warmth, rugby, and the phrase everyone eventually reaches for: I acted on your instruction.

The Managing Director

Lachlan Davis

Protects the structure by never touching the transaction himself. Decides, quietly and from a distance, who gets the right seat — and who only finds out at the stop.

The Labor Advocate

Lincoln Sloane

Appointed to represent Abi. Studies Novatrack through her case instead, and is standing closest when her appeal deadline quietly runs out.

Episodes

EP301 · 2000

The Better One

Two job offers arrive the same week: one modest and honest, one enormous and impossible to describe. Abi tells Thomas the pay is more than enough — but they can't buy her soul.

EP302 · 2000

New Shoes

Novatrack times its coffee breaks and notices a cleaner's shoes before it notices its own numbers. Abi learns what this company calls efficiency, and who in the building still means it.

EP303 · 2001

Seven Months

The financial system cannot produce a statement. Martin has been building the numbers by hand for seven months rather than admit the machine — or the man above him — is the problem.

EP304 · 2001

The Process

Bad debt is cycling through the same accounts, credited at month-end and reinvoiced the next. Abi follows it herself, account by account, and finds a pattern nobody meant her to see.

EP305 · 2002

The Right Seat

Abi is redirected to a fleet-management manual and a growing performance file. At the conference, Lachlan explains who gets to stay on the bus — and who only finds out at the stop.

EP306 · 2002

Recorded Twice

The capability hearing. Chloe says she acted on instruction. The official tape says something else by morning. Abi's own recorder remembers what the company's does not.

EP307 · 2003

In Good Hands

The labor lawyer appointed to protect her keeps her waiting outside his office until the appeal deadline passes, then calls it hopeless. She had the papers in her hands. He had the clock.

EP308 · 2003

Allowed to Retire

Sadie tells Lachlan what she saw. Helen reopens the tape and rebuilds what was erased. The people responsible are managed out quietly — resignation, retirement, reassignment. Nobody is fired.

EP309 · 2004

Is Anyone Looking

Novatrack has changed every name in the room and kept the system that failed them. Abi finishes the house, and Manon writes back: a foundation carries the wall whether anyone is looking or not.
